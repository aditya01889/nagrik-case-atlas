# Data and editorial policy

1. A pending allegation is not a finding of guilt. Keep allegation, investigation, charge, trial, conviction, appeal, acquittal, quashing, withdrawal, remission and sentence suspension distinct.
2. A politician's identity must be matched using reliable identifiers and source evidence; name similarity alone is insufficient.
3. A candidate affidavit is a dated declaration, not a live court-status feed. Keep affidavit declaration counts distinct from other verified case records discovered later.
4. Every public case record needs an original source link, source date, last-verified timestamp and verification status.
5. New documents and changed hashes are `discovered` / `needs_review` until a human checks the document. Automated monitoring never changes a legal conclusion on its own.
6. “Most serious case” is shown only when a reviewer has assigned a rank using a published, consistent classification framework. Display the underlying offence, legal status and source. Do not imply that the ranking establishes guilt.
7. No party-level criminality score, candidate recommendation or opaque overall score.
8. Do not count charges as separate cases. Keep related proceedings and appeals linked so totals can be audited.
9. Preserve corrections and previous versions. Do not silently overwrite records.
10. Missing data must be labelled as missing or not yet verified. Never translate an empty result into “no criminal cases”.
11. Location is opt-in. Use coordinates to find a polygon in the browser where possible, and do not store precise coordinates by default.
12. Provide a correction process and record the disposition of every correction request before broad public launch.
